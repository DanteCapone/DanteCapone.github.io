Research

My work sits at the intersection of physical oceanography, climate science, and observational data analysis. Below are my current and past research themes.

Ocean Mixing & Turbulence

Small-scale turbulent mixing controls the transport of heat, salt, nutrients, and carbon in the interior ocean. Despite its importance, mixing rates are poorly constrained globally. My work focuses on [specific aspect, e.g., quantifying diapycnal diffusivities from microstructure profiler data in the Southern Ocean].

Key questions I address:

  • What drives spatial and temporal variability in ocean mixing rates?
  • How do internal waves generated at topography dissipate their energy?
  • What are the consequences for large-scale overturning circulation?

Methods

Microstructure profilers · Argo float data · Fine-scale parameterizations · MATLAB & R data pipelines

Air-Sea Interaction

The ocean surface is the gateway through which the atmosphere and ocean exchange heat, momentum, and gases. I study how [specific process, e.g., storm-driven mixing events alter the seasonal thermocline and modulate air-sea CO₂ fluxes].

Key questions I address:

  • How do extreme weather events perturb the upper ocean heat budget?
  • What role does sea-surface temperature variability play in regional precipitation?
  • How can satellite observations constrain air-sea gas exchange estimates?

Methods

Shipboard meteorological measurements · Satellite SST/SSS · Reanalysis products · ERA5 · MATLAB / R
