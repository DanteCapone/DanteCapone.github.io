Research

My research examines plankton communities across gradients and extremes. I combine field sampling, molecular ecology, imaging, statistical modeling, and experimental approaches to understand how biological communities respond to environmental structure, disturbance, and measurement.

Plankton communities across environmental gradients

The California Current Ecosystem exhibits strong environmental structure across the coastal-offshore gradient, shaped by upwelling, water-mass mixing, and productivity. I investigate how zooplankton and phytoplankton communities vary across this gradient and which dimensions of biodiversity are most responsive to environmental change. This work integrates molecular, imaging, and environmental datasets to identify broad-scale community patterns and the taxa associated with distinct physical environments.

Methods: DNA metabarcoding · environmental datasets · multivariate community analysis · R statistical workflows

Molecular ecology and metabarcoding methods

DNA metabarcoding offers powerful resolution of community structure, but ecological interpretation depends on careful attention to methodological bias. My work evaluates how PCR amplification conditions influence inferred community composition and develops quantitative approaches to improve interpretation of metabarcoding datasets. I use Bayesian multinomial logistic-normal modeling and reproducible data workflows to connect method development with ecological application.

Methods: PCR cycle manipulation · 18S and COI metabarcoding · Bayesian compositional modeling · R (brms, Stan, vegan)

Wildfire impacts on plankton and coastal biogeochemistry

Wildfire is an increasingly important disturbance in coastal regions, yet its impacts on marine plankton remain poorly characterized. I study wildfire-plankton interactions using field observations, time series, and ash-addition experiments. This work examines how wildfire-related material and altered environmental conditions influence phytoplankton composition, productivity-related processes, and trophic interactions in coastal waters.

Methods: Long-term observational records · ash-addition mesocosm experiments · dilution experiments · phytoplankton microscopy · nutrient analysis

Observation, visualization, and interdisciplinary ocean science

I am also interested in how plankton are rendered visible through different scientific technologies and how those approaches shape ecological understanding. This includes work on visual communication, scientific illustration, interdisciplinary scholarship, and the interpretation of observation systems as part of ecological knowledge production.

Approaches: science communication · visual methodology · interdisciplinary writing
