Projects

Selected projects span ecological analysis, methodological development, experimental work, and interdisciplinary communication. Together, they reflect a broader interest in how plankton communities are structured, measured, and transformed across environmental gradients and disturbance regimes.

Wildfire-driven phytoplankton change in coastal California

Observational Phytoplankton Disturbance

This project examines how coastal phytoplankton communities responded to extreme wildfire conditions during the 2020 California fire season. Using long-term observations and multivariate analysis, I investigate changes in taxonomic composition, the taxa associated with wildfire periods, and the environmental context surrounding these shifts.

Correcting PCR-related bias in marine metabarcoding

Metabarcoding Bayesian Modeling Methods

This work evaluates amplification bias in marine zooplankton metabarcoding and applies Bayesian modeling to improve interpretation of compositional sequence data. The project combines experimental PCR-cycle manipulation with statistical modeling to better understand how methodological choices influence ecological conclusions.

Zooplankton biodiversity across the California Current Ecosystem

Molecular Ecology Biodiversity California Current

This project uses molecular and environmental datasets to investigate biodiversity patterns across the California Current Ecosystem. The analysis focuses on environmental gradients, broad diversity structure, taxa associated with distinct physical environments, and the ecological interpretation of regional community change.

Wildfire ash mesocosm and dilution experiments

Experimental Mesocosm Plankton Dynamics

Through ash-addition and mesocosm experiments, this project examines how wildfire-derived material influences plankton processes, including community composition and grazing-related dynamics. The broader goal is to identify mechanisms through which terrestrial disturbance may affect aquatic food webs and nutrient pathways.

Trait-based and size-structured views of plankton communities

Trait-based Size Structure Community Analysis

This project explores how shifts in marine communities are expressed through taxonomy, traits, and size structure. By connecting biodiversity data with ecological traits and size-based approaches, I aim to improve interpretation of community change across environmental gradients.

Visualizing plankton and oceanographic knowledge

Visualization Science Communication Interdisciplinary

This interdisciplinary project explores how plankton are represented through scientific imaging, illustration, and data systems. It reflects an ongoing interest in the relationship between ecological observation, visual culture, and scientific communication.
