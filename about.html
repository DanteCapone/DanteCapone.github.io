About

DC

I am a biological oceanographer interested in how plankton communities are organized across environmental gradients and how they respond to disturbance. My research sits at the intersection of marine ecology, molecular methods, field observation, and quantitative analysis. Across these approaches, I aim to understand when ecological change is expressed as shifts in community composition, size structure, or biomass, and how those signals are shaped by the tools used to observe them.

Much of my work has focused on the California Current Ecosystem, where strong cross-shore gradients in productivity, hydrography, and community structure provide a natural laboratory for studying plankton dynamics. I have used metabarcoding, imaging, trait-based approaches, and time-series data to examine biodiversity patterns across the region and to investigate how communities respond to episodic perturbations, including wildfire.

I am particularly interested in integrating complementary observation systems. Microscopy, imaging, and environmental DNA each resolve different dimensions of plankton ecology, and part of my research has focused on improving interpretation across these methods, including work on PCR-related bias in metabarcoding data. More broadly, I am motivated by questions that connect ecological pattern, methodological rigor, and interdisciplinary ways of seeing marine systems.
